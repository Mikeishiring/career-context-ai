# Select visual evidence

For new or substantially expanded context, investigate the work's visual evidence during research, before finalizing the account. Follow references to demonstrations, videos, photographs, documents, diagrams and physical work within the authorized source boundary. Do not stop at the first existing thumbnail or a list of links. No visual is required when none helps explain the work.

## Find the right original

Connect the artifact to a work account. Establish who made it, what the person contributed, the depicted version or event, and the intended audience. Distinguish the film's creator, uploader, people pictured and event organizer. A portfolio link or appearance in a film does not establish authorship. A colleague's recording may demonstrate shared work with explicit attribution.

Keep originals in place. Use an available authorized reader or a selected local file. When an original cannot be reached, record the exact missing artifact and the consequence; do not imply a poster, transcript or filename is a reviewed video. Reuse a reachable saved copy before requesting the same material again.

## Compare moments, then inspect the strongest candidates

For a video, first inspect its duration and available transcript/chapter information. Sample across its beginning, middle and end, then examine adjacent moments around useful actions or results. Compare a small candidate set rather than automatically selecting the first frame, midpoint or most colorful shot. For a short clip, viewing it through may be more useful than sparse sampling. An optional local helper is `scripts/sample_video.py`; it uses installed FFmpeg/ffprobe and never downloads a video. Missing tools are a reason to use another available viewer, not to invent a screenshot.

Choose based on what a reader can learn:

- Does the frame clearly show the particular work or decision being described?
- Is the meaningful state readable at the intended size, without menus, loading screens, motion blur or irrelevant overlays?
- Would another frame better show the action, result, comparison or person's contribution?
- Does it expose private names, contact details, internal data or identifying combinations?

Inspect shortlisted frames at full size. If sequence matters, use a useful before/after pair or retain the original video link; one still cannot prove a working interaction. Record the source locator, file hash when local, timestamp, selected version, the reason for selection and important rejected alternatives in private research notes. State whether review covered the full video or sampled moments. An extracted still is not evidence of adoption, performance or sole authorship.

## Carry the result into the document

Embed selected, genuine images in the related Work account using the existing artifact format. Identify the specific claim or part of the account the visual supports before writing its caption. Name the related work consistently so the connection survives a separate media section. Keep the source reference and any consequential scope limitation nearby. Use a short caption only where the image cannot convey that information. Check the embedded image, its enlarged state and phone-size legibility. Preserve the underlying original; do not create generated substitutes for evidence. Clearly label any requested reconstruction and keep it separate from actual work.

Compare the depicted state with the account's dates and scope. An older interface may show the origin of a project while omitting its newer workflows; a plan may explain a decision without showing completed work. Keep that limit beside the visual and avoid captions that imply otherwise. Do not use a historical image as proof of current counts, later capabilities or outcomes. If inspection weakens the claim, narrow the wording or retain the uncertainty; do not pick a more flattering frame to conceal it.

Private evidence is not automatically eligible for Profile. Public text and public visuals need their own deliberate selection and confidentiality review. The Profile exporter supports explicitly selected screenshots and HTTPS video links in `publicProfile.media`; use the [format](format.md). New media starts unselected. Selected screenshot bytes travel with the file; video links remain external and do not fetch thumbnails or play automatically. Withheld media and URLs must be absent from the public file, not merely hidden. Public captions and credits are authored separately from private filenames, source IDs, notes and excerpts. Inspect the chosen screenshot and the actual destination; check image contents, metadata and URLs for confidential information. Browser screenshot import re-encodes the selected copy to omit file metadata, but cannot remove sensitive text pictured in it. A visually compelling but unsafe frame can remain private or be omitted. Relabeling confidential material as synthetic does not make it shareable.

Prioritize visuals attached to the strongest consequential claims and changes in the person's work during the first run. Look across relevant accounts before choosing: a readable result, useful before/after or a demonstrable decision can explain more than an attractive landing page. Record why the strongest available candidate was chosen, the claim it helps the reader assess and what it cannot establish. If the strongest claim has no suitable visual, retain that gap rather than pairing it with unrelated decoration. This is a bounded evidence decision, not a requirement to fill a gallery or inspect every video frame.

## Finish the investigation

For each substantial retained account, record one of: selected visual and why; visual inspected but not useful/safe; original unavailable with a concrete next step; no relevant visual indicated by the sources. This is private research state, not a mandatory row or badge on the person's page. Stop searching once the chosen evidence communicates the needed point and no consequential candidate remains uninspected within scope.
