# Adapt to the person

Read what the person has already supplied before an intake question. Identify current work, desired work, seniority or scope when stated, hybrid responsibilities, the use of the output, and reference preferences. Treat these as a revisable working understanding, not a personality or proficiency assessment. Do not assign a discipline from a job title alone.

Use the optional `profile` fields to preserve disciplines, target roles, emphasis and reference preferences. They are free text, not a closed taxonomy. Keep the person's original accounts; tailoring changes emphasis and selection, never the truth of a claim. A general archive can cover several disciplines while a resume targets one role.

## Start with the work, then discover sources

Establish the decision the output should support: a role application, career change, promotion, interview, portfolio or open-ended record. Reuse the stated audience and objective. With no target, make a broad archive; create a general resume only when a resume is requested. Do not invent an ideal role from available records.

Map responsibilities, projects and periods before choosing platforms. Work may leave photographs, notebooks, rosters, repairs, client briefs, certificates, physical objects or oral accounts instead of tickets and dashboards. Sparse digital records do not imply weak contribution. Separate a credential from demonstrated practice, an unused proposal from a delivered change, and a reference resume's style from its owner's facts.

## Optional responsibility check

During onboarding, offer a short reflection from the supplied context: “Your title was X; the examples cover Y and Z. What have I missed or misunderstood?” Omit unknown details. Retain the person's account as attributed self-report; a title alone does not establish duties, authority, proficiency or results. Reuse known answers and keep collecting when the person skips this check.

Choose a useful follow-up when the available material leaves a consequential gap:

- What was already in place, and what did you maintain, introduce or change?
- Which decisions were yours, shared or subject to someone else's approval?
- What fell outside the title, or changed during that period?

These are exploratory prompts, not a required questionnaire. A role description or similar title can suggest a missing area to investigate. Keep that reference separate from the person's evidence; missing coverage remains unknown. Check actual responsibilities before searching for a baseline, and retain work that falls outside it. Do not infer impact or rarity from a job description, or responsibility from title prestige or employer reputation.

Keep the recorded title and period alongside a plain-language account of the work in the overview or timeline, with source references or attributed self-report. Familiar role labels may help explain overlapping work, but label them as comparisons; do not replace an employment title, assign seniority or invent a target role. Preserve changes over time instead of assigning the whole career one label.

In the guided document, the **Build context** starting note can include the role, responsibilities and decision boundaries. It remains a review note until the host reconciles it with the accounts and sources; filling it in or completing the step does not approve career facts. Ask only the unresolved questions that could change the account.

## Discover sources and guide the next step

Inspect the host's available connector metadata, supplied files and selected locations. Read-only availability discovery is not permission to ingest every account. Make a compact source map in `collection`: platform, exact location, access route, collection state, actual read scope and next useful step. Never mark a location read merely because search returned matches. Retain inaccessible replies, uninspected attachments, date gaps and pagination limits in scope/nextStep.

For each relevant source, use this order:

1. An available connector with suitable read access, within the user's scope.
2. A local export or file the user selected; inspect its actual format and preserve stable source locators.
3. A supplied account, selected excerpt or screenshot when originals cannot be reached.

If access is missing, name the relevant evidence and one actionable route: connect its supported read tool, select a relevant export, or supply an excerpt. Do not ask for passwords, install an integration silently, or send someone off to connect an irrelevant system. Host connectors and exports differ; report unsupported formats instead of pretending the renderer can ingest them. Convert selected source material into the documented context format yourself. For PDF, slides, documents or images, use the host's appropriate reader and inspect the rendered artifact when layout matters. Record unread content explicitly.

### When evidence is missing

Check authorized sources and known answers first. If a consequential gap remains, ask once: “Do you have access to [specific record]?” Explain only what it would help establish. If the person does not have it or cannot share it, offer two choices: describe the work in their own words, clearly attributed, or leave the topic out. Either choice is sufficient. Preserve the choice, continue with other supported work, and do not keep prompting or reopening the topic without new evidence or a new request.

Keep unavailable, unread and absent records distinct. None establishes that the person did not do the work or performed poorly. A self-report can describe experience; it does not supply an independently measured result. Do not manufacture metrics or substitute unrelated platform activity to fill the gap.

Only scope questions that affect access or interpretation should interrupt research. For example: “I can read the supplied project notes. May I also use the selected repository, or should I keep this to the notes?” Do not repeat settled permissions. With no connector, continue from authorized supplied material and disclose the missing original.

For repository and social-profile research, use [GitHub and social discovery](github-and-social.md). The existing resume is a starting account, not an exhaustive project list. Discover potentially relevant accounts across authorized sources before narrowing the story.

## Choose evidence that fits the work

These are starting lenses, not mandatory sections or platform requirements:

| Work | Investigate | Useful visual or work sample | Common overclaim to challenge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Talent / hiring | Intake decisions, assessment design, pipeline definitions, candidate experience, shared hiring ownership | Redacted scorecard, process map, scoped ATS export chart | Current recruiter assignment implies sole credit; offers are hires |
| Engineering | Constraints, design alternatives, implementation, review, incidents, adoption | Architecture diagram, interface, benchmark with method, selected code diff | Merged code proves use; a microbenchmark proves production impact |
| Operations | Handoffs, exceptions, controls, process use, cost or time records | Before/after workflow, SOP excerpt, measured cycle-time comparison | A written process proves compliance or saved time |
| Growth | Experiment design, cohort, baseline, attribution, durability | Funnel or experiment result with denominator and period | Correlation proves lift; small samples justify precise percentages |
| Marketing | Audience, positioning, creative decisions, distribution, measured response | Campaign creative, published page, brief, performance chart | Impressions are revenue; a team campaign is sole authorship |
| Business development | Partner fit, qualification, negotiation, stage changes, implementation, realized value | Redacted partner map, proposal, launch sequence, scoped pipeline | Pipeline is booked revenue; a signed deal is adoption |

For mixed or unfamiliar work, derive lenses from the actual responsibilities and evidence. Prefer concrete qualitative results to invented metrics. Source-authoritative exports establish counts; conversations can explain decisions and exceptions.

The six examples above are not a menu the person must fit. A theatre technician may need a cue sheet and repair log; care work may need a redacted handover or teaching observation; a craftsperson may need process photographs and commissioned objects. Derive questions from the actual work before naming a discipline.

Check coverage against responsibilities and periods, not source counts. Look for older, physical, routine, unsuccessful and less documented work as well as recent visible successes. Record consequential gaps and the next accessible source. A large message export is not a complete career record.

## Collect visuals deliberately

Follow [visual evidence selection](visual-evidence.md), including video sampling, candidate comparison, attribution and the public/private boundary. Inspect a relevant recording rather than treating its thumbnail as sufficient.

Look in referenced attachments, design files, decks, repositories, published work and supplied portfolios within scope. Inspect the actual artifact, its date/version and authorship. Choose a few that make the account easier to understand. Preserve permission limits, redact sensitive details before embedding, and exclude names or contact data the intended audience should not see.

Use an authorized PNG/JPEG/WebP/GIF under the input folder, with a meaningful title and a caption explaining any necessary scope or limitation. Images must remain understandable on mobile; a dense full dashboard shrunk to a thumbnail is not useful evidence. Prefer a focused crop or a faithful diagram when authorized and label reconstructions explicitly. Do not fabricate screenshots or use generated decoration as proof. Do not hotlink private images. If no suitable visual is available, retain a concise account and record the missing artifact as a next step only when it matters.

## Organize and challenge

Order the strongest relevant work first. Give accounts titles that name the work; use optional tags for themes and local search. Prefer a few distinct accounts to repeated accomplishments. A career switch should show transferable decisions with clear limits, not rewrite the former role as the new one.

For a single-account sheet, keep the person overview to a brief role or scope line. The account below carries the story; do not repeat its summary in the introduction. For several accounts, the overview should connect them without restating their results.

Before delivery, try to disprove the strongest claims: other contributors, prototype versus production, later reversal, sample/period, contradictory source, missing career dates, and artifact ownership. Ask what the records miss about personal meaning after drafting. Update the source account before regenerating a resume or interview draft. Structural validation cannot replace this semantic review.

## Test the selection, not only the format

Before drafting, compare episodes for relevance, distinctive personal judgment, support and breadth. Keep a short private selection note explaining why substantial episodes were emphasized or omitted. No fixed story count or universal metric requirement applies. Preserve the broad archive when tailoring; changing the target changes selection and language, never ownership, dates or outcomes.

When improving this process, test from raw material without preassigned disciplines or polished accounts. Include unfamiliar or hybrid work, sparse evidence, an unused proposal, and a reference belonging to someone else. Run the same evidence against two objectives. Check research choices, omissions, attribution and visual selection as well as rendering. Prefilled discipline fixtures test format compatibility only, not adaptive research quality.

For the responsibility check, compare questions derived from work alone with questions informed by the supplied title and scope. Include titles that understate or overstate the apparent remit. Check whether the added context exposes a useful unknown without importing expected duties, inflating authority or dropping work outside the role. Label authored walkthroughs separately from independent model trials.
