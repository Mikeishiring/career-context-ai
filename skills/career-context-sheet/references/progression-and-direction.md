# Progression and direction

Distinguish three things: **North Star** is the purpose or standard the person keeps working towards; **progression** is how their responsibilities, methods and scope changed; **direction** is what they want next. Use their own account and evidence. None proves the other, and a goal is not an achieved result.

## Find the purpose before naming the phases

Look for what the person was trying to improve, for whom, and what good work meant to them. A recurring purpose can connect changing work more naturally than job titles or a list of tasks. Prefer their own wording. When the connection is your interpretation, offer it as a tentative reading; do not invent a lifelong mission from a few projects. The purpose may change or have several strands. If no purpose is stated, leave a focused private question and continue with the supported episodes; a North Star is not required to complete the context.

Use that purpose to choose meaningful episodes and explain why the work changed. Connect the situation or constraint, the person's response, what changed in their responsibility or approach, and the observed result or remaining limit. Distinguish intended quality from measured quality, attempted scaling from achieved scale, wider participation from completed delegation, and broader contribution from formal authority. Describe growth positively where the evidence supports it; retain continuity, setbacks and unfinished attempts where those explain the journey.

## Progression from connected episodes

Before writing the profile timeline, compare earlier and later accounts for changes in responsibility, independence, judgment, collaboration, methods, scope and follow-through. A useful phase says what became different and links to the account that supports it. Keep setbacks, lateral moves, plateaus and unfinished work when they change the interpretation. Do not impose a promotion ladder, a career switch or continuous improvement on the evidence.

Choose periods that match real changes. Merge repetitive years into a meaningful phase when appropriate; retain precise dates in the private timeline. Keep a consistent date order across all authored progression entries, including unselected drafts, so including another period does not scramble chronology. Place overlapping or parallel periods beside the relevant span rather than appending withheld periods after the latest year. Repeating a role summary under five years does not show progression. A more recent tool or higher activity count is not automatically a stronger capability. Keep what continued alongside what changed. Do not force every project to serve one North Star, or describe every later phase as bigger or better. Increased judgment, shared ownership, specialization, recovery or a deliberate narrower focus may be the meaningful development.

When the person corrects the framing, retain their dated account as an attributed source, reconcile the affected private summaries and public progression, and preserve the earlier baseline and revisions. Their recollection can establish their purpose; it does not independently verify every result or precisely date a change within a broad year.

Write an initial supported account of the progression during the first complete run. Do not make the person ask repeatedly to connect the history. Preserve it as an interpretation where the connection is not explicitly stated; challenge it against contrary or later evidence before presenting it as settled.

## Direction from the person

Look for their own statements about interests, preferred problems, desired responsibilities, working conditions and what they want more or less of. Distinguish current preferences, older statements and your tentative synthesis. A successful project, job title, personality label or colleague's praise does not establish a desired next role.

If the current direction remains unclear, ask one focused question after showing a brief evidence-based understanding. Continue independent work while waiting. Do not manufacture a goal to complete a layout. An unanswered question stays private; omit an empty public Direction section. A draft based on older statements stays unselected until its currency is confirmed. Record the source/date in private notes, not as repeated public provenance copy.

## Keep three reading layers

1. **Exact account:** retain source wording, chronology, contribution, shared credit and unresolved facts in the private record.
2. **Selected highlights:** choose the most distinctive supported decisions, changes in responsibility, unusual combinations or outcomes. Routine activity need not receive equal emphasis. Compare potential highlights across the archive, including older and nontraditional work; explain selections privately.
3. **What it demonstrates:** state the broader supported capability or connection, with a concrete example close enough for the reader to judge it. Mark an interpretation as an interpretation. Never enlarge ownership, scale, adoption, causal impact or proficiency to make a bigger claim.

These are levels of emphasis and explanation, not three competing factual versions. Inspect the strongest claim more closely, including the underlying work sample and contrary evidence. Keep a material limit beside the highlight; putting it only in deeper disclosure must not make the summary misleading.

## Profile should help a reader judge fit

When a public draft is requested, compose the selected content as one reading:

- **Overview:** explain the concrete relationship across the work. Repeating broad role, skill or industry labels in the headline, overview and direction adds no understanding.
- **North Star and progression:** distinguish what the person wanted to improve from how they responded to changing circumstances. Show what continued as well as what changed; each phase needs a supporting episode, not a stronger-sounding title.
- **Examples:** order a small set that makes those connections understandable. Choose for supported relevance to the intended audience, not recency or prestige alone. Keep a less relevant example available as an unselected draft when appropriate; do not erase it from private context or omit evidence that materially changes a selected claim. Hybrid or parallel work can remain several strands.
- **Visual evidence:** connect each selected work sample to the recognizable example or phase it helps explain. Use the same work name where useful, and retain the visual's actual version and scope. An image of an earlier state does not demonstrate later capabilities. Follow [visual evidence selection](visual-evidence.md).
- **Direction:** state what the person wants next, when known, rather than repeating their history or converting past success into an assumed aspiration.

Keep the public reading concise. For an example, give the work and its consequential distinction first; deeper detail can explain decisions, outcomes, shared contribution and limits. These are content checks, not mandatory headings, captions or a fixed number of examples. Preserve material limitations in the summary if hiding them in a disclosure would overstate the claim.

Let the person choose which components and deeper details are included. Review the selected preview on its own: each claim and caption must remain understandable when other components are withheld. Do not automatically reinclude withheld material to repair the story. Explain that a collapsed section in an exported file is still shared; disclosure is a reading aid, not access control. Do not invent match scores or claim the reader's fit decision is established. A local export creates a file, not revocable access or an audience permission system.

## Check a fresh run

Start from raw material, without preset role/industry labels or polished output. Check whether a reader can follow the stated purpose, supported changes, selected examples and what the visuals actually demonstrate without needing the research notes. Unknown purpose or direction stays a precise private question; missing visuals stay an honest gap. Keep ongoing work, shared credit and setbacks. Compare relevant visual candidates rather than filling a gallery. When a public draft is requested, review its selected content separately from the fuller private account.

Challenge this with sparse, nontechnical or unpaid work: no promotion, measured outcome, digital artifact or desired next role is required. A sketch for cancelled work shows a proposal, not delivery; a photograph of a finished object does not establish who made it or that it improved an outcome. These are checks on inference, not examples to copy into a person's profile. A passing schema or attractive page does not establish good content decisions. Fix the missed decision in the governing instructions, not only in the demonstration's prose.
