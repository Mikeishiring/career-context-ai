# From records to a person's story

Use this after establishing work, sources and privacy. The agent owns the research loop within that scope. The person supplies memories, meaning and corrections; they should not have to repeatedly request another pass through known evidence.

## Give each pass a different job

Use these activities when they help answer the current question. Combine or reorder them and reuse sufficient retained originals; neither the table nor a pass count requires another search. A narrow update revisits affected evidence and dependent outputs.

| Pass | Work | Result to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| First account | Reuse prior context and corrections, map the selected sources, read the originals behind a useful episode and draft it. | A provisional account, its supporting evidence and the consequential gaps. |
| Deepen | Follow the most important gaps into decisions, alternatives, shared work, implementation and later outcomes. Inspect overlooked periods and reserve episodes. | Specific additions or corrections, with the sources that changed the account. |
| Challenge and consolidate | Try to disprove the strongest claims; compare omitted evidence with selected stories; check prose against originals and originals against prose. | A more faithful account, explicit limits and refreshed linked views. |

During those passes, connect the person's stated purpose to supported changes across episodes using [progression and direction](progression-and-direction.md), and investigate the strongest relevant [visual evidence](visual-evidence.md). In consolidation, read the overview, phases, examples and selected visuals together: each must add something concrete, and the depicted state must support the claim beside it. Unknown purpose, missing visuals and parallel work are valid; do not force a single career ladder or generate a public draft unless requested.

Then ask for the person's recognition and missing meaning. Incorporate their corrections and recheck the affected claims before handing off the same complete document. If feedback is pending, deliver a usable draft; keep that status and the pending question. Do not block other resolved accounts on an unanswered personal question.

The first pass may find the outline while missing much of its substance. Do not assign a percentage of career completeness, a confidence score or a quality increase from the number of passes. More detail is useful only when it changes what the reader understands. A later pass can narrow a claim, restore credit or remove a false connection without adding words.

## Keep the active context small

Choose one episode or a small related group and a few consequential questions per batch. Load the relevant original sections and surrounding context, not the entire archive or every skill reference. For a long record, read in coherent sections and save the position; do not silently truncate it or describe a partial read as complete. Keep aliases, dates, actors, state and exact supporting passages recoverable outside the working prompt.

Reuse the source index and compact continuation state, updating them when evidence changes. Search them before retrieving the same material again. Summaries help navigation; verify consequential claims against the original or retained exact text. Rewriting an earlier assistant summary is not corroboration. Do not strengthen a claim merely because several drafts repeat it.

After each batch, save what changed, what was checked without changing, and the next useful action. Before context pressure or a real run limit prevents a safe continuation, checkpoint and deliver the current draft with a concrete resume step. Never replace missing context with guessed dates, metrics, motives or causal links. A new session loads the checkpoint and relevant evidence, not a chain of increasingly compressed summaries.

## Keep a small, durable research state

For work spanning sessions, use `journey.investigation` in the same private packet and the [investigation loop](investigation-loop.md). Update it after each meaningful batch. Keep `journey.continuation` as the short resume pointer. When an existing evidence workspace already provides tool cursors and equivalent research state, preserve that workspace and record the dependency; do not create a competing research-state document. This is internal working material, not another user deliverable or a source of permission.

Retain:

- The agreed subject, work, locations, dates, exclusions and read budget, if specified.
- Stable account IDs, project aliases and unresolved identity matches.
- Per location: discovered, fully read and interpreted material; unfinished pagination; blocked originals and attachments. Preserve a durable recovery position when a cursor expires.
- Per consequential assertion: actor, action, state, event date, source ID and exact supporting passage or immutable span; interpretation and conflicting evidence separately.
- A ranked next-read queue. Each item says which account and question it serves, where to look, what could change, and whether it is pending, resolved, blocked or outside scope.
- Corrections, rejected interpretations, the current draft, its last checked version and the reason for stopping.
- Current pass and a short change record: question checked, evidence inspected, resulting correction or no change. Keep the next action specific enough for another session to execute.

Scale the checkpoint to the material. When exact evidence is already retained at stable source/section locators, reference it instead of copying it again. Keep corrections, selection decisions and next actions here; the original text and finished accounts belong in their existing stores. A small supplied packet should not need a larger parallel research report.

Do not retain an entire conversation when selected evidence will suffice. Respect the person's retention choices. If private working files are not authorized or available, use the current context and include a concise continuation note in the handoff; disclose that durable resume state was not saved. Reuse known answers and unchanged reads. Re-read when an original, revision, missing context or contradiction requires it.

## Run the next useful pass without another prompt

Alternate bounded source coverage with a question-driven read. Start with the most consequential missing or conflicting part of an account:

| Gap | Next useful read |
|---|---|
| The record shows participation but not judgment | Find the original proposal, decision discussion, options considered or boundary the person set. |
| A change is described without its reason | Find the problem it addressed, what alternatives were available and whose priorities drove the choice. Keep the person's input distinct from the team's decision. |
| Ownership is unclear | Read the assignment, the person's actions, colleague contributions and later handoff separately. |
| A promise sounds completed | Find delivery or execution evidence; otherwise retain the promise as planned work. |
| A launch sounds successful | Look for later use, support requests, corrections, abandonment and operational records. |
| A criticism or success lacks context | Read the surrounding exchange and subsequent resolution; check alternative explanations. |
| A career transition seems causal | Check the dates and explicit handoffs. Do not infer motives or causality from sequence alone. |

Read full conversations, nested replies and selected originals using the [source-research guide](source-research.md). Search both supporting and contrary evidence. An assistant's earlier summary is a lead: distinguish the user's account, a retrieved original, an assistant inference and a plan. A tool call or proposed edit is not proof of completion. A thread mentioning an email is not the email itself.

Save what changed in the account, including a finding that does **not** justify stronger attribution. Keep good work, mistakes, corrections and unfinished outcomes. Do not turn every uncertainty into either a flattering claim or an accusation.

Continue while there is a consequential, accessible next read within the authorized run. A polished paragraph, an exhausted first search page or a long source list is not a stop condition. After the challenge pass, if no material correction or consequential accessible lead remains, finish the current scope. Do not invent more searches to reach a pass count. New evidence or a correction reopens only the affected work and its dependent views.

Give every remaining gap a destination:

| State | Next action |
|---|---|
| Accessible evidence could change the account | Read it in the next bounded batch. |
| Original or permission is missing | Keep the affected claim qualified and name the specific source/access needed; continue other work. |
| Only the person can supply meaning or a correction | Ask one or two consequential questions; retain a draft while waiting. |
| A source or identity remains ambiguous | Keep the accounts or interpretations distinct and state what would resolve the ambiguity. |
| Tool fails | Retry only when the failure is plausibly transient, with a bounded retry limit; use an authorized cached original or selected export if available. Otherwise record the blocker. |
| User interrupts or an actual time, tool or context limit is reached | Save the current evidence and draft, remaining queue and exact resume action. |
| Scope is checked and remaining unknowns have no useful accessible lead | Deliver the current document with those limits; reopen when new evidence arrives. |

State the actual stopping reason; do not invent a budget excuse or silently expand the scope. Honor a supplied time or read budget and reserve room to save and hand off. This is foreground work, not scheduled continuation. The closing note needs only what materially changed, unresolved limits and the next action when one exists. Keep the detailed pass record private.

## Preserve events before choosing the story

Set the evidence boundary before ranking. For a bounded supplied packet, review each retained record for material episodes, or record a verified duplicate or a specific scope exclusion. An index preview or a search that found nothing is not that review. For a large or live archive, work through explicit coverage batches and retain uninspected areas as gaps; do not silently treat a convenient sample as the whole history. Where the material exceeds one working context, finish and save extraction batches before composing across them. Their handoff is source-linked events and read scope, not a polished story that decides the next pass's emphasis.

Use the existing private notes for a compact event index. **Extract before ranking.** While interpreting a source section, retain each distinct consequential action or decision about the person, including its stated reason. One conversation can contain several episodes; its title, opening topic or the question that led to it must not decide what survives. Record the actor, action, reason or unknown, event date, state and exact supporting passage. Separate interpretation from the source. A topic label such as "stakeholder coordination" cannot replace what someone actually decided or changed.

After a reading batch, compare the interpreted sections with the saved events before shortening them. Did an incidental exchange reveal a different responsibility, deliberate choice, correction or later use? Recover it now, even if it does not fit the tentative outline. Track a source's actual read scope separately from whether its material episodes were extracted; opening a file is not a coverage check. For a large archive, inspect overlooked periods and work areas as well as following questions about already selected work. Do not claim unread material has been reviewed.

Group events into episodes by the work and decision they concern, retaining aliases and links to later consequences or corrections. Do not merge distinct choices merely because they share a topic. Keep concurrent work visible and explicit connections distinct from hypotheses. Reuse existing source passages and account IDs rather than copying a second evidence store.

## Select for what the reader would otherwise miss

Choose the contributions before the narrative angle. Survey the episode inventory across periods and responsibilities, including work outside the title. A contribution is a particular intervention, responsibility or approach, not a broad topic. Several events can establish one contribution; two activities in the same area can reveal different choices. In a general career archive, the absence of a target vacancy is not a reason to discard work.

Before outlining, save a compact contribution map in the private notes. For each consequential candidate, record **what the reader should understand**, its supporting event IDs, its evidence boundary and its destination. State the understanding concretely: what problem or audience the person served, what they personally changed or chose, and why that choice matters to the account. Record missing reasons as unknown. A label such as "teaching" or "process improvement" is not enough. This is a selection aid, not a second evidence store or a form to show the reader.

Decide **materiality** separately from **claim strength**. Materiality concerns what omission would change about the reader's understanding of the person's responsibility, initiative or working approach. Claim strength determines whether the work can be described as proposed, built, used or measured, and whose account supports it. A documented contribution can matter without measured impact or adoption. Conversely, a large metric or vivid criticism need not define the person. Missing outcome evidence does not by itself decide whether a contribution belongs in the account.

Choose two layers separately. Keep material contributions understandable in the **first read** or **supporting depth**; then choose which best explain the person at first reading. Compression can move a contribution into depth without erasing its account. Use **archive only** for repetition, incidental activity, insufficient evidence or agreed exclusions, with a specific reason. "Already covered" must identify the retained contribution and the same understanding it preserves. Sharing a topic, tool or audience is not duplication. Do not give every event a paragraph or impose a quota by area.

Compose from that map. Introduce the work's purpose, audience and personal contribution before its qualifications; integrate shared credit, actual state and limitations where they change the account. A correction can reveal judgment, but must not become the reader's only explanation of what was created. Choose a through-line that fits the selected contributions rather than keeping only work that fits an attractive arc.

Challenge both transitions before polishing: **source to inventory**, then **contribution map to prose**. For a broad or mixed history, begin the challenge from the evidence without reading the draft or its selection map: record the important distinctions, then compare them with the writing. Use a fresh reviewer when available and authorized; otherwise identify this as a separate self-review, not an independent check. Resolve disagreements against originals and the reader's purpose. A different preferred emphasis does not require another story; a missing responsibility, audience or reason may.

Revisit originals behind broad notes and strong omissions, including material read for another question. Compare an informative omission with a weaker retained passage, applying the same materiality test to achievements, criticism and routine work. For each material contribution, quote the actual sentence or sentences that convey the understanding selected before drafting. Check whether named work has an understandable purpose and personal contribution, not just a status or caveat. A topic mention, citation or raw record does not count as a readable account. Repair missing meaning or explicitly reconsider the selection; do not silently redefine it to match the draft. For a handoff, compare announcement, actual transfer and later continued involvement.

Keep these notes private and proportional. For a small addition or correction, update the affected contribution and check its account; reuse the existing map and leave unrelated selections intact. Reopen the broader selection when new evidence changes the overall picture. The sheet contains the story and useful depth, not the selection machinery. A fully assigned inventory or a matching quote proves neither sound judgment nor complete meaning. Personal significance may still require the person's account; ask about that distinction instead of pretending the records determine it.

## Build episodes before a career through-line

For each useful episode, connect the situation, stakes, choices, personal action, shared contribution, outcome and later understanding. Use the existing account fields; an unknown detail can remain empty. Do not require a dramatic turning point or a numerical result.

Draft the connected account in `summary`; preserve the evidence and detail in `context`, `aim`, `contribution`, `decisions`, `sharedCredit`, `outcome`, `limits` and `sourceRefs`. Keep assertion-level provenance, corrections and rejected interpretations in the private packet using [memory and retrieval](memory-and-retrieval.md). The account-level source list is a reading aid, not a substitute for those links.

Only then propose a through-line in `person.overview`: recurring ways the person works, changes in responsibility, and what they say those changes meant. Link a proposed pattern to the episodes supporting it and check exceptions. One episode supports an episode-level observation; it does not establish a lifelong trait. Avoid forcing concurrent work into a linear career pivot. Do not infer personality, motives or identity from sparse activity.

Use first person for a clearly labelled draft the person can correct. Do not fabricate quotes or make assistant-written prose look like the person's original words. Personal meaning can be supported by an attributed self-report; it need not be independently witnessed.

## Ask for recognition and missing meaning

Reflect a short draft and ask one or two questions that would materially change it. Examples:

- "The sources show the handoff and later correction. What judgment did you make at the time that those records miss?"
- "What changed in how you approached the work afterwards?"
- "Which part of this account feels unlike your experience, or gives someone the wrong share of the credit?"

Do source-answerable research first. Do not demand metrics, insist on a success story, or repeat the intake. Record a correction, inspect conflicting sources where available, revise visibly and preserve unresolved disagreement. Never silently convert a draft into `person_reviewed`; that state requires the person's actual review.

## Check the story in both directions

**Prose to evidence:** For every consequential sentence, check who did what, whether it was offered, assigned, implemented, used or measured, when it happened, what each source establishes and what remains uncertain. An exact quotation can still fail to support its claim.

**Evidence to prose:** Re-read the compact assertion notes. Did the account lose a material personal decision, another contributor, a mistake, a correction, a later outcome or the scope of a number? Include it or record a defensible reason to leave it out. "Already covered" is insufficient when the retained sentence says something different.

Use the selected originals or cached full text when recovering evidence. Do not cite from memory. No additional retrieval is necessary when the relevant evidence is already present; the repair may be to the narrative.

Before handoff, compare visible prose, copied text and embedded JSON. Deliver one private HTML sheet with the relevant limits and next factual questions. Source coverage, semantic review and the person's recognition are separate checks. None alone certifies a complete life story.

## Enforced flow in the local research server

The integrated server provides 450 source calls and 1,600 requests. This capacity supports bounded extraction and event review; it is not a minimum duration. Prefer consequential questions over extra volume. The general skill works without the server, but then these obligations are instructions rather than executable checks.

1. Use `research_probe` when a new read could change the account. Choose its question, source group and lens label. The listed lenses are suggestions; there is no source quota, minimum probe count or prescribed search order. Retained originals can already answer the question.
2. Read the originals and finish known discovery. When the server reports `retention.protocol`, use `extraction_packet` and `save_extraction` to review every cached section, then `episode_packet` and `episode_inventory` to retain each event in a selected or reserve episode or a specific exclusion. Selected episode IDs become account IDs. The host derives source notes; `save_notes` is for legacy sessions. Draft, check and save the first plan.
3. Challenge the strongest claims against relevant contrary evidence, shared ownership and later outcomes. Reuse retained originals; make a fresh search only when it could change the result. In retention sessions, compare the strongest reserve episode with a selected account and resubmit the inventory after the first plan. Revise the draft and plan as needed.
4. Submit `depth_review` with the relevant questions for each account, using suggested lens names or your own. Every narrative claim still needs represented evidence and review. Cite exact spans and real probe receipts when probes were used. Record a consequential counterpoint or limitation; do not create filler categories.
5. Map every material saved attribution to claims carrying that exact actor/action reference, or explain the omission. Check whether the saved note itself omitted a contribution from the original. A citation to the record cannot substitute for narrative credit.
6. Review `turning_point_packet` including reserve events. In v2 sessions, `save_turning_points` requires importance, exact `prose_checks` and `qualifying_event_ids`. Essential events cannot be omitted; their qualifications must survive in the same account. Evaluate the through-line separately: a cross-episode pattern needs at least two grounded accounts and an exception or limit. Quote the actual overview's interpretation and limit in the depth review, so the connection reaches the reader. One episode cannot establish a career-wide trait.
7. Resolve accessible pending questions before finishing. Unknown personal meaning, excluded sources and genuine evidence gaps remain explicit. New evidence, notes or a changed plan invalidate the prior review.

The review is performed by the researching agent. The checks validate process and references, not whether the interpretation is true. Do not label it independent or person-reviewed. The local checklist saves per-account findings and gaps; the portable sheet retains questions and limitations while keeping the working audit private.
